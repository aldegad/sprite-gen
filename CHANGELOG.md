# Changelog

All notable public changes to `sprite-gen` are recorded here. Versions track the `version:` field in `SKILL.md` and `pyproject.toml`.

## v2.43.0 - No loop delivers a ghost frame unmeasured; a cross-fade inside one silhouette is named, not judged

- **Fix: no loop path delivers a ghost frame unmeasured — `video-cycle-align` takes a clean source frame beside it.** A video model filming a walk held on twos or threes sometimes leaves a frame half drawn between two drawings: a band of part coverage where a leg was and is going. The alignment took such a frame as filmed at its own time, never measured, and a frame RIFE made beside it carried the band and passed — `--between auto` judged a made frame only against its two neighbours (`dark_excess`, `outline_loss`), and a neighbour that is itself the ghost leaves nothing in excess. Now every frame delivered is read on its own coverage (`rife.ghost`: part-covered pixels, alpha 0.1–0.9, in regions at least 5 px thick, over the frame's solid pixels; a ghost over 0.3 %, `interpolation_quality.GHOST_WARN`). The source loop is screened first (`ghost_screen` per loop row and in `cycle_align`: `limit`, `thick_px`, the loop's own `level`, `reads`, and each filmed `ghosts` frame of `cycle.source/`); a loop whose median frame is over the limit is drawn part-covered (a glow, a translucent cape) and is not judged. Under `auto` and `nearest` a time on a filmed ghost takes the frame after it (or before it), a made frame that is a ghost gives way to the nearer source frame (fault `ghost`), and where that nearer frame is the ghost the other frame beside the time is taken; `rife` keeps both, named. Each such cell is listed in `ghost_at` (`at`, `source`, `ghost`, `taken`) with a warning line; a ghost with no clean frame beside it is kept and the line says to film the direction again. A loop already the set's length is screened the same way. Made frames in `smear` carry their `ghost` reading.
- **The same screen in the other loop paths.** `video-loop` records the screen of the cut it delivers for a walk or run (`ghost_screen` in its report and summary, a `warning:` line per ghost naming `cycle/frame-NNN.png`): a loop delivered as cut, never aligned, shows its ghost. The jump repair rejects a proposal that is a ghost, its rounds' `original` and `proposal` carrying `ghost`. `video-loop-compare` and `video-loop-repair` keep their versioned `rife.smear` measures and do not read it. Not seen by the screen: a made frame that blends two drawings inside one silhouette with its outline whole — no part coverage to read; the cross-fade reading below names it. A loop the screen does not read (`reads: false`, drawn part-covered) is now said on stderr too: `video-loop` prints a `warning:` line for its cut (and carries `ghost_screen` in its summary), and `video-cycle-align` a line in `warnings` per such loop — before, only the report said so.
- **Named, not judged: a made frame that cross-fades two drawings inside its silhouette.** Where the legs trade places across a gap wider than the flow follows, RIFE can move the coverage and cross-fade the colour — both drawings' boots at part strength inside one whole outline. That frame has no band of part coverage, nothing darker inside the body, and loses its outline only where the art outlines in near-black: the outline rule counts pixels under 70/255 as outline, so the same cross-fade outlined lighter than 70/255 (grey, a light brown) passed every fault — caught or passed by the palette's accident. `rife.crossfade(made, a, b, t)` reads it relative to the source frames' own edges: in windows 8 % of the body's height where the two source frames differ, pixels at their plain blend at the made frame's fraction (in patches 5 px thick) together with the made frame keeping under 60 % of the sources' strong edges there (local range over 7 px, wider than RIFE's blur) — the strong-edge pixels lost there, over the frame's solid pixels (the windows step by half their size, so one lost pixel is counted in up to four of them). Over 0.015 (`interpolation_quality.CROSSFADE_LOOK`, `looks`) a made frame is named to look at: `cycle_align.smear` rows carry `crossfade` and `look`, and a kept frame gets a `warnings` line ("may cross-fade two drawings inside its outline … kept"). It is no fault — `auto` keeps the frame and the retake rule does not count it — because checked against frames labelled by eye it does not part them: on synthetic legs over 32 palettes (outline black, brown, grey or none; boots dark, mid, light or none) the colour-blended, coverage-whole frame reads over the line in 209 of 384 (74 of 96 outlined in grey, where the fault rules catch 2; 11 of 96 drawn without outlines) and a clean drawing between two far-apart drawings in 29 of 384. The jump repair takes such a proposal and names it (its round's `look`, the proposal's `crossfade`, a `video-loop` `warning:` line). `video-loop-compare` and `video-loop-repair` keep their versioned measures.
- **`tests/video/test_crossfade.py`** draws the outlined legs with boots three times the size, the outline grey or black: a frame whose colour run cross-faded inside the drawing's own coverage reads over the line in both, the outline rule catching only the black; the drawing between reads under it, and a clean drawing between legs 13 degrees apart in grey over it (why it is no fault); the alignment keeps a cross-faded frame and names it, where the black one still gives way to its outline fault; an alignment through a stand-in whose colour run blends names each kept frame in a warning line; the jump repair takes a cross-faded proposal and names it; through the real RIFE, where installed, a short step reads no cross-fade; and a loop drawn part-covered is named on stderr by `video-loop` and in the alignment's warnings. `tests/video/test_rife.py` pins the real RIFE's whole verdict on the melted crossing (`outline` and `ghost` one way, `outline` the other, no cross-fade either way), where it pinned only the first fault.
- **`tests/video/test_ghost_screen.py`** draws outlined legs with a band of 0.3 coverage between the feet: the measure reads the band and not a softened edge; the alignment takes the clean frame beside a ghost at a whole time and beside a made frame that carries one, keeps it named under `rife` or with no clean frame beside it, screens a loop already that long and leaves a loop drawn part-covered as before; `video-loop` names the ghost in its cut; the jump repair rejects a ghost proposal; and through the real RIFE, where installed, no resampled cell carries the filmed ghost.

## v2.42.0 - A set's directions on one sheet, standing on one ground line; a loop whose strip as cut is missing is refused by name

- **New `sprite-gen video-set-sheet`: the directions of one set on one sheet, standing on one ground line.** Each direction is cut on its own and a strip is the union of its own cycle's bodies, so laid side by side the directions stand at different heights and cell sizes. `video-set-sheet --loop-dir <dir> --view <view> …` (the views as `video-cycle-align` takes them; a strip turned over to face the other way is a direction like any other) writes `<name>.sheet.png` and `<name>.sheet.json`: a row per direction in the compass order S, SW, W, NW, N, NE, E, SE whatever order the loops are given in, a frame a column; one cell that holds every pixel of every cell of every direction, centred on the pivot each strip declares (`anchor`) or else its bottom centre, so every cell's pivot is its bottom centre; one ground line, `baseline_y`, the lowest body pixel (alpha 8 and over, as `video-loop` reads a body) of any cell — a fainter pixel, a soft shadow, is kept in the cell below the line and is no body — (a direction whose strip leaves room under its feet stands that much above it, as cut: `body_bottom_y`); and every row as cut, pixel for pixel — nothing is scaled direction by direction, one size across a set is `video-loop --body-height`'s. `--center <still>` stands a transparent still (the set's base) with its body on the same line at the rows' standing height (the median of their `body_h`, against the still's own as `video-loop` reads one), centred, in `<name>.center.png`. Loops of different lengths, in frames or frame time, are laid all the same, the shorter rows' last cells empty, with `same_length: false` and a warning. The record carries `order`, `columns`, `cell_w`, `cell_h`, `baseline_y`, `anchor`, `same_length`, per row its `code`, `view`, `frames`, `delay_ms`, strip `name`, `input`, `strip_sha256`, `body_h` and `body_bottom_y`, and the centre's `png`, `input`, `sha256`, `standing_h`, `body_h`, `scale` and `box`. A direction given twice, a front or back view with a side, a `--view` count other than the loops', a loop directory without one strip and its sidecar, an opaque centre still and a centre beside rows without `body_h` are refused before anything is written. The loop directories are only read. Every other verb writes what 2.41.0 writes. [docs/video-pipeline.md](docs/video-pipeline.md#7-one-sheet-for-a-set--video-set-sheet).
- **`tests/video/test_set_sheet.py`** lays synthetic strips of different sizes, one standing above its strip's bottom and one with a declared pivot: the rows come out in compass order from any order given, every cell is its strip's cell pixel for pixel with no alpha lost, the lowest body pixel of any cell is the ground line and the floating direction stands its gap above it, a soft shadow under the feet is kept below the line with no alpha lost, the cell is the bodies' union about the pivot and touches it where they do, and a declared pivot lands on the cell's centre. A sheet laid by hand — every strip centred in one wide cell and stood on its bottom, the ground line the lowest pixel of any cell — is the engine's sheet cropped by one offset, cell for cell, its ground line the same line. A centre still drawn twice the rows' size and wider than them, with a faint shadow, stands its body on the line at the rows' median standing height, centred, the cell made to hold it and its shadow. Rows of 12 and 10 frames, or of one length at two frame times, are laid and said to differ; each refusal writes nothing.
- **Fix: `video-cycle-align` refuses a loop whose strip as cut is missing, by name, before any loop of the set is touched.** Since 2.41.0 a loop's first alignment reads its strip as cut (`<name>.strip.png`, or `follow.source.png` where a follow-through moved it) to record `cycle_align.origin`. A loop directory holding `cycle/` and its `strip.json` without that PNG ended in a `FileNotFoundError` traceback, with no report and `cycle.source/` already copied into every loop of the set. It is now refused as a loop cut before `cell_height_cap` was recorded is: `video-cycle-align: <loop dir>: no <name>.strip.png — …`, exit 1, every loop of the set file for file as it was (under `video-set`, that state's alignment fails and its loops stay as cut). It is still not aligned without its origin, which `video-follow --read-on` carries regions by: put the strip back, or cut the loop again. A loop aligned before keeps the origin it recorded and is aligned again without its strip, as before. [docs/loop-repair.md](docs/loop-repair.md) section 4.
- **`tests/video/test_cycle_align.py`** takes the strip out of one loop of a synthetic set: the refusal names it, and both loop directories are as they were, with no `cycle.source/` and no report. A loop aligned once is aligned again without its strip, its origin kept.

## v2.41.0 - Regions read on the cut follow the loop aligned from it, cell for cell; a floor on the stretch, by direction and never above the fold rule; the bend and the seam written down

- **New `video-follow --read-on <sha256>`: the strip the regions were read on, and the reading carried from it.** The regions of a follow-through are where somebody looked, in the first cell of the strip they were read on. `video-cycle-align` turns a loop to start on a foot strike, so its first cell is another, and `video-follow` given the same regions read the motion again from that cell 0: the regions landed on another place of the body, and a stretched loop moved them by a motion read anew. `--read-on` names the strip they were read on by its file's sha256 (as cut, before any follow-through). This loop's own strip as cut is `same`: the follow-through is the one without the flag, byte for byte. The cut this loop was aligned from is `transported`: each cell is carried by the cut's reading of the cut's cell it is, the regions stay on the cut's cell 0, and the damped mass is solved on the loop's own order, length and frame time — a turned loop gives the cut's follow-through cell for cell, a loop stretched by the nearer frame the new cycle's offsets on the cut's motion. A cell made between two frames of the cut, or one that is not the cut's cell pixel for pixel (a crop or a scale changed and nothing records how, or a cell drawn again), is refused `uncertain`: a made cell has no reading of its own, and none is taken from the nearer frame by rounding (a cut made before its strip recorded its crop is judged by its cells' pixels alone). Any other strip is refused `no-match`. Without `--read-on` nothing of this is read or written and the record holds no new key; with it the record carries `read_on` (`sha256`, `relation`, and for `transported` its `policy` and `cut_cells`). `video-follow-inspect` takes it too. [docs/video-pipeline.md](docs/video-pipeline.md#regions-read-on-the-cut-followed-on-the-aligned-loop----read-on).
- **Output change: `video-cycle-align` records the cut it aligns from and where every cell is from.** `cycle_align.cells_from` holds, per cell of the rebuilt strip, `{"source": i}` (frame i of `cycle.source/`, as many times as it is taken) or `{"between": [i, j], "t": t}` (a frame made between two); the report's loop rows carry it too. The first alignment of a loop also records the strip as cut, read before anything is rewritten (`cycle_align.origin`: its file's sha256 — `follow.source.png`'s where a follow-through had moved it —, cell size, scale, crop origin, `sample_indices`, each cell's pixels by sha256, and the body's motion as `video-follow` reads it there), and every later alignment keeps it as it is. The strip, cycle frames, GIF and WebP are unchanged; the aligned meta gains these two keys. A loop aligned before this release has no origin and gets none: its cut is no longer there to read. [docs/loop-repair.md](docs/loop-repair.md).
- **New `video-follow --stretch-floor D`: a least area every moved pixel keeps, read by the way the region moves.** The fold rule keeps a region from folding and so lets its steepest pixel take nearly nothing of the source. A move (ox, oy) of a cos² region leaves 1 − (π/2)·hypot(ox/rx, oy/ry) of a pixel's area; each region takes the largest gain, in steps of 0.01, that keeps D in every cell, and never one above the fold rule's — where the direction would allow more (a region moving along its long axis) the fold rule's gain stands, so the floor only lowers. Under it the fold rule's handling stands: a region taken under 1 is held and named, every region held is refused, and `--on-fold refuse` refuses a move under the floor. It is opt-in with no default value: without it the fold rule alone decides, byte for byte, and a floor the move already keeps draws the same strip, GIF and WebP. The record carries `stretch` (`policy`, `floor`, per region `per_gain`, `least_cell`, `rule_gain`, `floor_gain`, `gain`, `held`, `least_area`, `under_measured`), and stderr names what lowered a region, the fold or the floor. On an aligned loop, with `--read-on`, the floor is read on the cut's motion, so a turned loop is lowered, held or refused as the cut is. [docs/video-pipeline.md](docs/video-pipeline.md#a-least-area-each-move-leaves----stretch-floor-d).
- **Output change: `video-follow-inspect` writes schema 2 — the bend and the seams beside the least area.** A move stretches the picture (`jacobian_min`, D), tilts a line by its steepest slope, |offset|·π/(2·smaller radius) (`bend`, G, with `bend_deg` = atan G; the same number as `fold_ratio`, named for what it does — at 1 a line tilts 45°), and where two regions overlap bends it where the larger move changes hands. `cells[k].seams` and the record's `seams` give, per pair of moving regions that meet, the largest jump of the move's gradient on that curve and its angle (`jump`, `deg`, the point `at`, the `cell`); a held region has none. `regions[i]` adds the largest `bend` and `bend_deg`. `inputs.settings` adds `read_on` and `stretch_floor`, `inputs.policy` the names of the ways a reading is carried and a floor kept, both in `input_id`; and `carry_basis` says what carries a region (`outline-lay`) and that whose pixels it holds is `unverified`. `video-follow`'s own record changes only where a new flag is given, and then also carries `carry_basis`. [docs/video-pipeline.md](docs/video-pipeline.md#inspecting-a-follow-through--video-follow-inspect).
- **`tests/video/test_follow_read_on.py` and `tests/video/test_follow_stretch_floor.py`** use synthetic strips. A runner turned by `video-cycle-align` and followed with `--read-on` is the cut's follow-through cell for cell, and the same turned loop followed as before moves other pixels; a loop stretched by the nearer frame is carried cell for cell, duplicates and all; made cells, a crop that narrowed when a frame was dropped and a cell drawn again are refused `uncertain` with the loop left as it was; another clip and an unknown or malformed `read_on` are refused; a floor on a turned loop decides as on the cut. Every cell keeps the floor; where the fold rule is stricter its gain stands, where the floor is it lowers; a floor already kept draws the same bytes, and a floor of 0 is the fold rule, not the direction alone. A lowered round region bends a line just under 45°; two overlapping regions record a seam at the point where their weights are equal, its jump the norm of the gradients' difference there. Two strips alike in alpha whose chest patch moves differently are carried alike and say the region is unverified, and an arm added to the outline carries nothing.

## v2.40.0 - A restored cell that brings back the jump the repair took out is not an improvement

- **Output change: source restoration's comparison reads the jump around the restored cell (`axes.jump`).** A cell the restoration rebuilds is one the jump repair replaced, because a step into or out of its source frame jumped. The source frame's pixels clear the in-between's melt and can bring that jump back, and the comparison read only the melt. `video-loop-compare` (and the preview in `video-loop-repair`) now scores the final cells of both loops with the jump repair's own step score (`repair.jump_scores`: per step, the change of coverage over the whole body and over the hair behind it, each over its median in the loop, the larger of the two), on the source cells' union box and with the `facing` the loop report's `jump_repair` recorded. A changed cell reads the larger score of the step into it and the step out of it; more than the baseline's is `regressed`, reason `jump:regressed`, as on every other axis. No bound is read (`JUMP_RATIO` included) and no constant is added: the baseline is the measure. A cell the jump repair made is now restored only where its in-between jumped more than the source frame does, as when it lost coverage; where the in-between was the smoother step and only its drawing melted, the delivered in-between stays. The guard reads coverage, not colour. `axes.jump` carries both loops' step scores and their whole-body and hair parts, the facing, the hair box, the box and, per changed cell, its two steps and both readings. A loop report whose jump repair names replaced cells but no facing (`right` or `left`) is shared `unknown`, `jump-repair-facing-unverified`; a loop whose repair replaced nothing needs none and stays a no-op. [docs/loop-comparison.md](docs/loop-comparison.md#the-jump-guard).
- **Contract versions: comparison schema 5, metric `source-restoration-v4`, policy `key-protected-source-copy-one-step-cap-jump-guard-v1`.** The repair report stays schema 3, and a restored cell's pixels are made as in 2.39.0. Evidence and receipts of `source-restoration-v3` (2.39.0) are refused, never read as v4: a v3 repair report given to `video-loop-compare` is an input error, and a request whose baseline carries a v3 receipt in its loop report is shared `unknown`, `restoration-receipt-policy-unsupported`. The origin itself is still a valid first request. Proposal IDs change with the versions.
- **`scripts/dev/source_restoration_demo.py`** still ends with both requests `improved` and the third a no-op, its 25 checks unchanged: each melted in-between there left the feet a pale ghost, a larger jump than the source frame's. Its stand-in loop report now records the jump repair's `facing`, as `video-loop` writes it.
- **`tests/video/test_source_restoration.py`**, facing right and left: a restored cell whose source frame stands six pixels to one side of its neighbours clears the melt and is `regressed` with `jump:regressed` alone, while the other proposal stays `improved`; a restored cell that gives the steps back the source's reads no higher than the in-between; the axis is the jump repair's own score on the source cells' box. A record without a facing, or with another word, is shared `unknown`, and a loop with nothing replaced is still a no-op. Receipts and evidence of v3 are refused.

## v2.39.0 - A damaged cell is restored from its own source frame; a faulty in-between is no longer adopted; a follow-through is shown in every cell

- **Output change: a jump repair adopts RIFE's in-between only when it has no interpolation fault.** In a walk or run loop (`video-loop --repair auto|on`), the frame that breaks a jump used to be replaced by whatever RIFE drew between its two neighbours. RIFE's frame is now a proposal, measured against those neighbours with `rife.smear` and adopted only without a fault: a smear inside the body (`dark_excess` over 0.001), a lost outline (`outline_loss` over 0.05) or an empty frame keeps the filmed frame. These are the bounds cycle alignment already used, now one policy for both (`sprite_gen/video/interpolation_quality.py`). The limit of 3 (`MAX_REPAIRS`) counts interpolation calls, rejected proposals included; a rejected target is not tried again in that run, and a target next to a frame already made is skipped while an independent one remains, where it used to end the search. The report's `jump_repair` adds `attempts`, `blocked` and, per round, `target`, the `original` and `proposal` measurements, `faults` and `outcome` (`accepted` or `rejected`). A rejection keeps the filmed frame and its jump: it is not a verdict on the loop. [docs/loop-repair.md](docs/loop-repair.md).
- **Output change: a loop records the source it was cut from.** `video-loop`'s report adds `source` (one sha256 over the ordered keyed PNGs' own sha256s, their count and the fps, read before cleanup, size hold and anchoring), `producer` (a fingerprint of the measuring code), for a walk or run `gait` (cut-local silhouette signals, `status: "unverified"`: they do not identify the character's own feet), and under `--anchor none` or `body` `source_projection` (the cleanup recipe, the body ramp and each frame's pixel digest before the jump repair). The strip's meta adds `sample_indices`, `source_rect` (null under `--anchor feet`) and `source_cut` (the source, the cut's start and length, and the source frame of each cell). The cells, the GIF and the WebP are what 2.38.0 writes but for the jump repair above.
- **New `sprite-gen video-loop-compare`: the loop shown now against a candidate, read from the final files.** It reads each side's final strip and meta (after `video-follow`, if that ran) and its loop report for provenance only, never for a seam score or a pass, and writes a `sprite-gen-video-loop-comparison` (schema 1, `same-sample-defects-v1`): the sha256 and size of every file as given, a digest of the decoded cells, and per axis the two values and whether they compare. It orders only defects measured at the same source samples, timing, crop and scale: smear and outline loss frame by frame against each frame's actual neighbours, without averaging a worse frame away, and the seam over the native canvas; a worse axis is `regressed`. `improved` also needs every sample's coverage byte for byte the same, and colour changes only in frames that failed the interpolation bounds and now pass them. Another cut, an aligned strip without a proven source mapping, missing or inconsistent evidence and changed coverage are `unknown`; equal pixels and timing are `non_regressing`, never `improved`; the gait stays `unverified`. Every verdict, `unknown` included, exits 0; invalid input, disagreeing geometry and I/O failures are errors. An application that replaces a result on its own should take only `improved`. [docs/loop-comparison.md](docs/loop-comparison.md).
- **New source restoration: `video-source-manifest`, `video-frames --source-manifest`, `video-loop-repair`, and `video-loop-compare` given the origin and the source.** A cell the loop report lists as made by the jump repair, which carries a smear or outline fault that the source frame at the same time does not, is rebuilt from that source frame, with no interpolation. In that cell a pixel that differs from the source becomes the source's, unless the source's pixel would raise the key-colour guard there: where that excess is exactly one step that the cell's resample made out of source pixels with none, the source's pixel is written with the keyed channels one step lower and its own coverage; anywhere else the delivered pixel stays, and if that leaves a coverage the source does not have, the proposal ends `unknown` and writes nothing. Nothing is blended, moved or taken from another frame; the cut, the metadata bytes, every other cell and the GIF and WebP schedules stay as delivered. `video-source-manifest` (or `video-frames --source-manifest` with `--reference`) receipts the clip, the canvas, the frames report, the ordered keyed PNGs and the clip's presentation timestamps. `video-loop-repair` takes the current five files (strip, meta, loop report, GIF, WebP), the origin's (the five the first restoration started from; on a first request the same five again) and the source, and writes one candidate into a new `--out-dir` and a repair report, for one of up to three independent cells (`--proposal-index`). Every request rebuilds the current loop from the origin and the source and recomputes the receipt kept in the loop report rather than trusting it. `video-loop-compare` with the same origin, source and repair evidence measures the five final files again: `improved` needs the one changed cell to be exactly that copy with the source's coverage, a measured fault cleared, no other cell, metadata byte or playback interval changed, and no protected axis worse; key excess is counted per pixel, so less of it elsewhere does not hide new spill. A correspondence the engine cannot prove exactly (legacy evidence it cannot explain, a follow-through, cycle alignment, size hold, motion anchoring) is `unknown`, not a guess; source, origin or evidence files that are missing or changed are an error. Only a final `improved` is grounds to adopt a candidate. Repair report schema 3, comparison schema 4, metric `source-restoration-v3`, policy `key-protected-source-copy-one-step-cap-v1`; evidence of the two unreleased drafts (`source-restoration-v1`, `-v2`) is refused, never read as v3. The cell resample is unchanged and caps nothing; it now publishes the source pixels each cell pixel is mixed from (`resample.mix_windows`), which the restoration reads. [docs/loop-comparison.md](docs/loop-comparison.md#restoring-the-active-cut-from-source).
- **A synthetic example, end to end: `scripts/dev/source_restoration_demo.py`.** It draws a small walker (twelve keyed frames at twice the cell's size, and a clip of them over green for real timestamps) and a stand-in for a delivered loop whose cells 4 and 9 are in-betweens that melted: outline and two-colour belt drawn in the body's colour, feet a pale ghost. From there only the verbs above run: `video-source-manifest`, two `video-loop-repair` requests, each compared `improved` by `video-loop-compare`, the second on the first's adopted files with the same origin, and a third that is a byte-exact no-op. It ends with an error unless every other cell, the metadata bytes, the GIF and WebP schedules and the origin's files are unchanged, each restored cell has the source's coverage and its outline fault cleared, no key excess is introduced, and the belt's one-step excess, which the resample makes where its two colours meet, is capped in each restored cell. Its `summary.json` holds no path or time. It is a new synthetic example, not a measurement of any delivered loop; the GIFs below are its two engine GIFs with each pixel drawn 3 × 3.
- **`tests/video/test_loop_compare.py`, `tests/video/test_source_restoration.py`, `tests/video/test_loop_repair.py` and `tests/video/test_strip_resample.py`** use synthetic loops. Identical pixels are never `improved`; an outline restored with the bob kept is `improved` and its inverse `regressed`; a follow-through is measured from the final pixels; another cut with a better report ratio, a flattened pose and flattened colour motion cannot win. Restoration keeps normal motion and timing, offers the next independent proposal within the budget, is a no-op on a clean loop, refuses forged or changed source receipts and tampered final files, keeps key spill out while restoring the rest of the cell, ends a proposal whose protected pixel has another coverage, caps a one-step excess the resample made and keeps a deeper one or one a source pixel carries, and rebuilds every later request from the origin. A blocked worst jump does not hide an independent one; faulty proposals keep the originals and use the call budget; with the real RIFE (`tests/video/test_rife.py`) an in-between that loses the outline is rejected and the filmed middle frame kept. The resample's published windows hold every pixel its colour filter mixes, and a mix can carry more key hue than the colours under it.
- **New `sprite-gen video-follow-inspect`: where a follow-through carries each region in every cell, with nothing moved.** `video-follow` is given an ellipse in the strip's first cell and carries it with the body into every other; inside the carried ellipse every pixel moves, whatever it is a picture of. For the first cell somebody looked. ## v2.12.1 - The error body is masked as the provider sent it, before it is put on one line

## v2.12.0 - A refusal names the provider's code, and says when it was the content policy

## v2.5.0 - API image generation and smoother loop cuts

- New `openai` image provider: `sprite-gen gen --provider openai` calls the OpenAI Images REST API with nothing but `OPENAI_API_KEY` — the credential a headless container (a Modal worker, CI, a SaaS backend) can have, where the `codex` route's interactive ChatGPT login cannot exist. - sprite-gen stays subscription-first. `codex` and `grok` run on a subscription you already pay for; `openai` bills per call, so it runs **only** when `--provider openai` names it. `SPRITE_GEN_DEFAULT_PROVIDER=openai` is refused rather than honoured, the guided `workflow` flow neither offers it nor saves it as a preference, no availability fallback targets it (a codex outage still reaches grok, never metered credit), and having `OPENAI_API_KEY` in the environment changes no route by itself. - `gen --quality low|medium|high|xhigh|max|auto` and `gen --resolution 1k|1.5k|2k`: the two knobs an image is billed on, as one shared vocabulary with a per-provider subset. grok Imagine carries `auto|low|medium` and all three resolution tiers and prices an image on the pair; openai carries the whole quality range and takes its size from `--aspect-ratio`; codex `image_gen` exposes neither dial. A knob a provider cannot honour fails loudly instead of being dropped from a request body you are about to pay for. Omitting both keeps every existing call byte-identical. The resolution names are tiers, not pixel counts — `1.5k` rendered 1408x1408 at 1:1 (2026-09-20 measurement) — and the grok subsets were read off the server rather than the prose: `quality` deserializes the wider shared enum and then refuses per model (`high` → HTTP 400 "This model only supports the following quality value(s): low, medium, auto."), `resolution: 1.5k` renders although the capability guide lists only 1k and 2k, and an unknown field is not refused at all, which is why these names are checked locally. Grok image reports carry `extra.quality` and `extra.resolution` when the request sent them.
- `video`, `video-extend` and `video-edit` announce the per-call charge on stderr when they run on `XAI_API_KEY` instead of the Grok subscription login — one line per submitted job, naming the duration and resolution it is priced on. The subscription route stays silent and no other video behaviour changed.
- Registering a provider now fails loudly when the workflow catalog has no label for it. The guided flow built its provider choices by zipping labels onto the provider tuple positionally, so a third provider was dropped without a word; labels are a checked mapping (a missing one fails at import) and the flow offers the subscription routes only. `workflow` access probing knows `openai` — ready when the key is set, with `billing: api-credit` and the billing confirmation that goes with it.

- `video-loop` automatic cuts now score the transition actually played from the last displayed frame back to the first. The chooser prefers a step comparable to the cycle's ordinary motion rather than the smallest distance, which could repeat a pose and pause at the wrap. Reports retain `next_frame_distance` as a separate diagnostic.
- `video-frames --spill full` now lowers the tint threshold as well as lifting the cluster-size limit, so faint key-colour reflections are corrected. `--spill auto` examines the reference at the same threshold before selecting full correction, preserving the character's own key-coloured material. The conservative `small` mode and the default 3-second video-set duration are unchanged.

- Gait auto-detection now considers an ambiguous double-period candidate even when the short candidate exceeds the duration floor. It preserves both phase occurrences at source speed when the longer local minimum is similarly credible and passes the periodicity gate; near-exact short repeats, worse doubles, non-gait states and caller window bounds are preserved. Reports identify this conservative choice with `half_period_guard.reason = "ambiguous-harmonic"` and `cycle.review_recommended`, rather than claiming anatomical stride certainty.
- Added a [loop review guide](docs/loop-review.md) defining what numeric gates establish, when sequential visual review is needed, and how to record explicit cut/alignment choices without presenting them as automatic decisions.

## v2.4.1 - A visible hop is no longer refused as "never leaves its rest pose"

- `video-loop` one-shot detection no longer refuses a visible hop because the rest pose is not one pose. The excursion is admitted by either the peak's height in MADs of the rest noise (as before) or the fraction of the subject's pixel mass the peak moves (new, ≥ 0.4); the report records `excursion_moved` and `excursion_rule`. A body that walked a few steps, hopped 42 px and froze scored 1.8 MADs because the walking preamble and the frozen tail inflate the "rest noise"; by moved mass it scores 1.07. A jittering stand still fails both rules, and clips the MAD rule already accepted are cut exactly as before.

## v2.4.0 - Three-second clips and gait windows in seconds

- `video-set --duration` defaults to 3 seconds (was 6). A repeating motion holds two or more cycles at 3 s (walk strides measured at 0.6–1.3 s across seven bodies), a single action is one hop or swing that the one-shot cut already handles, and 6 s bought only a longer wait — and, for jump, more idle standing between hops. `--duration 6` still asks for a longer clip.
- `video-loop` walk and run take their period window in seconds (walk 0.5–1.6 s, run 0.3–1.2 s, capped at half the clip) instead of a fraction of the clip length. At 3 s the old 6–31 % window topped out at 0.96 s and put a 1.0–1.3 s stride out of reach: on four walks generated at 3 s it refused one outright and cut another at a half step; with the bounds in seconds all four pass with equal or better seams, and the 6 s results are unchanged. Idle, jump and attack keep their windows.
- The strip sidecar (`<name>.strip.json`) records `kind` (`periodic` / `one-shot` / `fixed`) and `loop` (`false` for a one-shot), and the spec asset loader reads `loop` from it, so a jump or attack cut as a single action plays once on a trigger instead of repeating. A sidecar without the key is a loop, as before.
- The GIF/WebP never ask for more frames than the strip has cells. A cycle longer than the 64-cell cap is subsampled into the strip; the GIF/WebP are cut from those cells, so requesting the cycle length repeated cells back to back, the writer merged the identical neighbours, and verification refused its own output ("64 frames, expected 68" on a 2.8 s jump cycle).

## v2.3.1 - Key reflections removed from video sprites

- `video-frames --spill small|full|auto` and `video-set --spill` (default `auto`): remove the key colour a video model paints into the subject — a reflection across metal, a tint on a pale surface — which the chroma engine's trapped-spill pass leaves alone once the patch is larger than its small-cluster cap. `auto` keys the still the clip was made from and measures its own key-coloured material; none worth the name means every key tint in the clip is spill (`full`), otherwise the still-pipeline rule stays (`small`). Colour only — alpha is unchanged — and colours without key tint are untouched. `video-frames` defaults to `small`, byte-identical to before; the frames report records the decision under `spill`.
- Known limitation: on pixels where the reflection is removed, colour can lean warm. The correction solves observed = (1-k)·subject + k·key for the subject and so scales the unkeyed channels by 1/(1-k), which amplifies any warm light already mixed in (a pale armour piece can come out slightly reddish instead of neutral silver). A correction that keeps the source still's palette is open for contributions.

## v2.2.1

- `video-loop` gait half-period guard: a `walk`/`run` period shorter than the state's floor (`LoopProfile.min_seconds`: 0.6 s / 0.35 s) is treated as one step of a two-step gait and doubled when the doubled period repeats within 25 % of it — the case where a costume hides the legs and the half period dips as deep as the full one, which the depth rule alone returned as a one-legged loop. A straight line fitted to the body's centre across the cycle carries the drift and not the step; only that line is removed and every cell stands on the mean foot line. - `video-canvas` / `video-set`: `cheer`, `wave` and `celebrate` now take the wide canvas (raised limbs leave a 1:1 frame at the top corners), `video-set --shape` forces one shape for a whole batch (wide costumes), and `video-set` carries `cheer` / `wave` motion templates that name no limbs.
