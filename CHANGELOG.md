# Changelog

All notable public changes to `sprite-gen` are recorded here. Versions track the `version:` field in `SKILL.md` and `pyproject.toml`.

## Unreleased

- **Docs: a clip from a video MCP on your agent goes through the set stage and the size hold.** That route stopped at `video-loop`, so what `video-set` does for a set was left out. It now cuts a walk or run with `--anchor motion-auto` (the anchor that holds a clip with no end frame at its first frame's size; `video-loop`'s default for a walk, `body`, does not), takes `--fps` from the frames report, and ends with `video-cycle-align --view …` once a walk or run is cut in two or more directions (`SKILL.md` routes, [docs/video-pipeline.md](docs/video-pipeline.md#a-clip-from-a-video-mcp-on-your-agent--zcre) with `Set` and `Size` rows in its ZCRE table, [docs/user-workflow.md](docs/user-workflow.md), the READMEs). `video-cycle-align` is in the pipeline's diagram and verb table. `SKILL.md` forbids replacing any engine stage — extraction, the loop cut, set alignment, loop repair — with a script of your own: the engine's own options first, the defect reported, a new clip only for what no stage can reach. [docs/qa-motion.md](docs/qa-motion.md) says its "regenerate that row" is for image rows; a video loop is repaired and aligned by the engine first. No code or output changes.

## v2.25.0 - Every view of an aligned walk starts as the same foot lands, read off the legs, and RIFE leaves no black between crossing legs

- **Output change: every frame RIFE makes, and where every aligned loop starts.** Frames made by RIFE (`video-cycle-align`, `video-set --align-cycles auto`, `video-loop` jump repair) are drawn differently wherever the legs cross or the flow is unsure; frames taken as filmed are untouched. Every loop `video-cycle-align` turns may start on a different frame: on its legs instead of its top edge, and, with views, on one foot for the whole set. A loop already aligned starts where the new rule says the next time it is aligned (it is read from `cycle.source/`).
- **Fix: a black smear between crossing legs in a frame RIFE made.** RIFE runs its flow twice, once for the colour and once for the coverage, and where two legs pass each other the runs disagree: the coverage says body where the colour still carries what lay around the body. 2.24 premultiplied the colour over black, so the disagreement came out black. The colour image now carries the body's own colour around the body (`rife.bleed`: the colour from 0.8 % of the body's height inside its solid edge, past a drawn outline, pushed out over the transparent area), so a disagreement reads as the body's fill; inside the body the colour is the frame's own, outline included. On two outlined legs walking through each other the made frame at t = 0.5 had 6 % of its body dark beyond either neighbour; now none (`tests/video/test_rife.py`). A limb RIFE cannot follow is still soft and pale, never black. [docs/loop-repair.md](docs/loop-repair.md#1-rife--what-runs-where-and-what-it-costs).
- **A smear is measured on every made frame, never passed over quietly.** `cycle_align.smear` lists per made frame `dark_excess` (dark pixels inside the body beyond the darker neighbour's count — a moved hat or watch keeps its count) and `partial_excess` (part-covered pixels beyond the more ragged neighbour's); over 0.1 % dark, the frame is named in the alignment report's `warnings`, on stderr and in `video-set`'s `warnings` (`rife.smear`, `align.SMEAR_WARN`).
- **New `video-cycle-align --between nearest`** (`video-set --align-between nearest`): a time between two source frames takes the nearer source frame, so nothing is made and no RIFE is needed; the motion keeps the filmed frames up to half a frame off their time. `rife` stays the default. A loop stretched this way shows a frame twice; its GIF and WebP are checked for one frame per run of identical cells, since both hold a repeated frame as one of twice the delay.
- **Fix: the loop started where an ear tip was lowest.** The turn took the frame whose alpha box top was lowest, and a long ear, a hat's point or an antenna owns that edge and flops on its own rhythm, so each view started at another point of its step. The start is now read off the legs (`align.foot_strike`, `turned_on` in the loop row): side and diagonal views on the widest stride of the foot band (the lowest 8 %), a heel landing in front; front and back views on the lowest solid row, the foot nearer the viewer drawn lowest; and only for a body with no legs to read, the body's top line, the first row at least half as wide as the frame's widest, so a narrow ear is passed over. With `--view` (and in `video-set`, always) the view picks the signal and the swings only say whether there are legs: a side or diagonal view where the foot band swings by 15 % of the body's height or the lowest row by 1.5 %, a front or back view where the lowest row swings by 1.5 %. A short-legged walk misleads a threshold either way — from the front the foot behind lifts out of the foot band, which then swings like a side stride mid-step; from the side a short step opens the feet by less than 15 % — so the thresholds choose the signal only without `--view`: the stride where it swings by 15 % or more, otherwise the lowest row where it swings by 1.5 % or more. A body that hardly bobs turns on its legs as one that bobs does (`tests/video/test_cycle_align.py`: a drawn walker whose ears are lowest half a step off its heel strike, with and without a bob; a big-headed, short-legged walker from the front and from the side).
- **New: every view starts as the same own foot lands** (`video-cycle-align --view front --view side@right …`, one per `--loop-dir`, `--start-foot right|left`, default right; `video-set` passes each item's view and facing). A walk lands twice a cycle, half a cycle apart, and until now the turn took either. The foot is told by how the view draws it (`align.strike_foot`, with `handedness.placement` for which own side is where): front and back by which foot is drawn lower (nearer the viewer), side and diagonal by which leg is shaded (the far one). Where the cue does not follow the step (its once-a-cycle swing under a quarter of its spread) or the two strikes do not differ enough, the foot is not named rather than guessed: the loop starts on its larger strike with `start_foot: null` and `foot_why`, and the report's `warnings` say so; without `--view` the report says once that no foot was chosen. Each loop row carries `view`, `start_foot` and `foot` (the cue, its values and margin). [docs/loop-repair.md](docs/loop-repair.md#4-one-cycle-for-a-direction-set--video-cycle-align-video-set---align-cycles-auto).

## v2.24.0 - A walk filmed from its first frame loops at one size, a frame scaled up keeps its crown, and a soft part can follow the body

- **Output change: a walk or run loop whose clip changes size by 1 % or more is cut differently.** `video-loop --anchor motion-auto` now holds the clip at its first frame's size before the cycle search (`--size-hold auto`, the default; `size_hold` in the report): the subject's fitted height over the clip is measured (`gait_fallback.scale_drift`) and, at 1 % or more (`SIZE_HOLD_MIN`), every frame is scaled back to the first frame's height about its fitted foot point (`undo_scale`). Until now that ran only in the gait fallback, after the search had failed and at 3 % or more, so a clip that grew 1–3 % and still looped was cut on frames of changing size: the frame one cycle on was larger than the first, and the loop popped at the wrap. A clip under 1 % is cut exactly as before; `--size-hold off` gives the old cut. A clip that grows 2.5 % now loops at its size where it found no cycle before (`tests/video/test_gait_fallback.py`); a front catwalk filmed from its first frame (+2.0 %) went from a seam ratio of 1.44 to 1.19. [docs/video-pipeline.md](docs/video-pipeline.md#4-loop--period-first-seam-second-then-the-gait-floor).
- **Fix: scaling a frame back up cut off what it carried past the frame's edge.** A clip that shrinks is scaled up about its feet; when the feet also rose in the frame (a back walk going away), the crown landed above the frame and was cut in every frame it reached. Frames are now widened by the room the furthest one needs (`gait_fallback.undo_padding`, the same for every frame, `padding_ltrb` in `size_hold` and `gait_fallback`), so nothing is lost; a clip that needs no room keeps its frame size and bytes.
- **New `sprite-gen video-follow`**: a soft part of a looping body (a chest, a belly, a pouch on a strap) follows the body's motion. An ellipse in the strip's first cell (`--region cx,cy,rx,ry`, repeatable) is moved as a damped mass on the body's own bob — the crown's row and the head's middle read off the cells, the periodic steady state of x'' + 2ζωx' + ω²x = −body'' per harmonic of the cycle (`--freq` 2.4 Hz, `--zeta` 0.6, `--gain` 2.5 times the physical answer) — so it lags and settles with no kick, and the last frame leads into the first. Only the region's pixels change; a move that would fold it over is refused. The strip, GIF and WebP are rewritten and checked, `follow` is recorded in the strip meta, and the strip as it was is kept as `follow.source.png`, so a second run never moves a moved strip. The region is the caller's to give: the engine does not find it. [docs/video-pipeline.md](docs/video-pipeline.md#6-follow-through--video-follow).
- **`video-cycle-align` clears a follow-through**: it rebuilds the strip from the cut, so it removes `follow.source.png` and the `follow` record and says `follow_cleared` in the loop's row; a new `video-loop` cut removes them too. Run `video-follow` after the alignment.

## v2.23.0 - A walking side view shows the far wrist's item each time that arm swings forward; without `--handed` every prompt is 2.22.0's

- **Without `--handed`, every prompt is 2.22.0's, byte for byte.** Clips (built-in or `--motion`, every model and pin), `video-prompt`, stills (view, facing, reference, key line, layout guide, the `--facing-fix regen` regeneration), `video --direction side` and the mid-step redraw send what 2.22.0 sent. `tests/gen/test_prompt_freeze.py` holds every prompt string in that table to `tests/fixtures/prompts-v2.22.0.json.gz`, drawn from the 2.22.0 tag's source. The engine's pieces go on through one place (`sprite_gen/gen/prompt_parts.py`, `Prompt.add`), which puts them on as 2.22.0 did; with `--handed` the prompt is that same prompt with the handed sentences put in, and nothing else changes. [docs/prompt-assembly.md](docs/prompt-assembly.md) lists the faults of the 2.22.0 prompts kept until a before-and-after comparison says to change them.
- **Fix: a watch on the far wrist was in no frame of a side walk.** 2.22.0 said every item on the far side of a side view hidden, and never named it in the clip ("the wrist in front of the body stays bare"). A walking character's far arm comes forward of the body with every step, and what its wrist or hand wears shows then. An item on a wrist, a hand, a forearm or an elbow (`handedness.limb`, one table: `SWINGING_ARM_PARTS`) on the far arm is now said to show wherever that arm is out in front of the body: in the still ("wherever the far arm reaches out in front of the body, clear of its outline, the black smartwatch shows whole on it; where the body covers that arm, it is hidden") and in a walk or run ("stays on the wrist that wears it in the image, on the arm on the far side of the body, and shows each time that arm swings forward; the arm nearer the viewer stays bare, exactly as in the image"). On the near arm the walk says the other arm stays bare "both where it shows behind the body and where it swings out in front of it". Both start "Both arms swing back and forth with each step, opposite to the legs." (`handedness.ARMS_SWING_TEXT`, the one place that sentence lives), and no sentence holds an arm still. An item anywhere else — an ear, the head, a tail, the body, a leg, a shoulder, the upper arm — gets no arm sentence and keeps the 2.22.0 sentences, as does a state that does not step (idle, attack). [docs/video-pipeline.md](docs/video-pipeline.md#handedness--an-item-on-one-side).
- **A side view names the near part "nearer the viewer", not "in front of the body"** (still and clip sentences): in a mid-stride still the far arm is the one in front of the body, so "the wrist in front of the body stays bare" named the wrist with the watch.
- **A clip keeps what its first frame shows**: for a walk or run with an item on the far wrist or hand of a side view, `video-prompt --json` (`still_needs`, and stderr) and `video-set` (each item's `still_needs`) say the still must be drawn mid-stride with that arm out in front of the body and the item in view. The docs give the pose sentence for the prompt.
- **One handed sentence per wrist, not per item**: with an item on each wrist a side clip said "the wrist in front of the body stays bare" and "the red ribbon stays on the wrist in front of the body" together, and with two items on one wrist it said the same sentence twice. Items on one part share a sentence, a part another item is on is not called bare, and a still says "none of it" there. A diagonal's clip sentence also reads "…, nearer the viewer, for the whole clip" (the comma was missing). A start still's prompt handed back to `gen --direction --handed` carries the handed sentences once.
- **`handed-check` passes a far wrist's item in view and fails the near arm's** (side views): 2.22.0 failed every frame that showed the far item whole ("in full view on the far side"). An item on a far wrist, hand, forearm or elbow must now be out in front of the body in every frame it shows — on the facing side of the body's centre by at least a tenth of the body's height (`far_in_front`); at or behind the centre it is on the near arm. With the new `--state walk|run` it must also show in at least a quarter of the frames (`far_shown`; unchecked without `--state` or for a state that does not step). A side view's near item must show in three quarters of the frames, not half: a far item turned over to face the other way shows in about half. Any other far item keeps the hidden-or-a-sliver rule (`far_hidden`), and a diagonal's near side keeps half.
- **The caller's own words are reported, not edited.** Text that turns the subject another way than `--facing` (or to a side in a front or back view), or puts a `--handed` item on its other side, is sent as written with a warning; text that repeats an option gets a note. `gen` prints them and records `extra.prompt_notes`; `video-prompt --json` lists conflicts in `warnings` and all of them in `notes`; `video-set` records `prompt_notes` per item. The prompt is the same with or without them.
- **`tests/gen/test_prompt_assembly.py`** draws every prompt in the option table as text — views, facings, items on a wrist or an ear, what the caller's text already says, states, clip models, the start still and the hand-offs between verbs — and checks each for the prompt without `--handed` plus the handed piece and nothing else, a handed sentence said twice, a limb called bare and dressed at once, an arm sentence where no wrist item is or no step swings it, and the notes.

## v2.22.0 - A character with an item on one side has its left-facing views drawn, not mirrored, and an attack is filmed with less room above

- **Handedness, said once** (`sprite_gen/gen/handedness.py`): `--handed "<item>=<left|right> [part]"` names an item on one of the character's own sides ("the black smartwatch=left wrist"); the engine works out where that side is in each view — the picture's right or left in a front, back or diagonal view, near or far in a side or diagonal view (`placement`) — and says it in the prompts (`text`). `sprite-gen gen --direction <view> [--facing …] --handed …` adds the engine's still view sentence and, per item, which side it is on, where that is in the view, and that the sentence outranks an attached picture; with `--handed`, `--facing-fix mirror` is refused and `regen` never falls back to a mirror. `video-prompt --handed` and `video-set --handed` end the clip prompt with where the item stays, anchored to the first frame and named once where it shows; on the far side of a side view it is not named at all — the near arm "stays bare, exactly as in the image" — since a clip prompt that names a hidden item draws it on the near arm. [docs/video-pipeline.md](docs/video-pipeline.md#handedness--an-item-on-one-side).
- **Left-facing views drawn, not mirrored — opt-in**: `video-set --facing right,left` films every side and diagonal view both ways, each from its own still (`--base side@right=… --base side@left=…`); a turned view missing a still for one facing is refused, naming the `gen --direction … --facing …` call that draws it. Items are named `<view>-<facing>-<state>`, the table names the facing, each side still is inspected for its own facing, and gaits are aligned across all eight. With `--handed`, a front or back walk's mid-step redraw is told the item's side as well. Mirroring stays the default (drawing costs as much again); one facing keeps the item names and table as before.
- **New `sprite-gen handed-check`** checks a still or loop frame by frame: a colour-marked item (`--marker`, a saturated colour nothing else on the character has) must sit on its view's picture side of the body's centre, in one place only, showing in at least half the frames on a near side and, against a `--reference` front or back picture, no more than a sliver on the far side of a side view and more than a sliver on its near side — a side view has no picture side, so a far sliver turned over to face the other way does not count as the near item shown. Without a reference both size rules are reported unchecked (`far_hidden`, `near_whole`). `--strap --zone` adds the item's band seen without its marker (a bare strap on the other arm) within the rows it sits in. Specks of the marker's hue left on outlines by a clip's colour blocks are recorded, not judged. `--board` draws every frame with its verdict; exit 1 on a failure. Drawn test figures pass as they should look and fail turned over in every view, a side view's far sliver against `--reference` (`tests/qa/test_handed_check.py`).
- **Fix: a diagonal facing left was filmed heading right.** The diagonal walk and run sentences said "to the right" and "toward the upper right" whatever `--facing` was; they now take the facing ("heading diagonally toward the viewer and to the left"). `video-prompt` and `video-set` also cut a left-facing diagonal's loop with `--facing left` (it was always `right`), which mirrors the jump repair's hair box. Right-facing prompts are unchanged.
- **A `gen --transparent` run with `--ref` keeps the alpha it comes back with**: `auto` still plans a chroma key before the model runs (2026-09-08: codex drew a checkerboard in 5 of 6 ref runs asked for alpha), but the raw is read before the key is applied (`chroma.classify_raw_alpha`). A raw with an alpha band, at least 5 % of it at alpha 0 and at least half of its border at alpha 0 is published on its own alpha through the native check, with `alpha.strategy: "native"` and `alpha.strategy_source: "refs-attached-raw-alpha"`. Keying it read the RGB under alpha 0 as the background and took the outline and light fills off: a transparent reference is answered with alpha, and a fox redrawn from one lost its outline and cream belly. A checkerboard, a key background or an opaque RGBA (no alpha band, or no alpha 0) is keyed as before (`refs-attached`); alpha 0 that misses either bar is keyed as before with a `warning`. `alpha.raw_alpha` records the verdict and its numbers on every ref run that planned the key. `--alpha-mode chroma` keys whatever comes back. [docs/gen.md](docs/gen.md#transparent-output--strategy-per-provider).
- Measured 2026-10-04 on codex `image_gen` (subscription), one transparent front still as the reference, three takes per arm: with the planned key's prompt, a transparent reference came back with real alpha 3/3 and the same still on white came back on opaque white 3/3; asked for alpha (`--alpha-mode native`), both came back with real alpha 3/3, with no checkerboard in 6 runs. The step down is kept until a larger measurement replaces the 2026-09-08 one.
- **The step down asks for the key it plans**: a `gen --transparent --ref` prompt that names no key background gets the `--chroma-key` key's line (`chroma.KEY_BACKGROUND_TEXT`, moved from `video.batch` so a clip's mid-step redraw and this run say the same sentence); a prompt that names a key — its hex code, or "magenta/green background", "chroma key", "screen" — is left as it is, with no second line. `alpha.key_background` records `injected` and the key. A reference on white with no key in the prompt came back on opaque white (3/3) and keying it took the outline and the cream fills; with the line, five takes (three on that reference, two on the transparent one) came back on a magenta key and kept both. `--alpha-mode chroma` and native runs are unchanged. [docs/gen.md](docs/gen.md#transparent-output--strategy-per-provider).
- **`--decontam` is skipped, not refused, on a ref raw that came back transparent**: it has no key to remove, and the refusal came after the paid generation. `alpha.decontam` records what was asked and why it was skipped, and stderr says so (a warning for `palette`).
- `chroma.classify_raw_alpha` judges on the pixel counts, not the rounded percentages: a raw with alpha 0 under 0.005 % was `no-alpha` and keyed with no warning; it is `ambiguous` now.
- **An attack canvas keeps 20 % of its height above the still, not 35 %** (`STATE_CANVAS["attack"]`; 28 % in front and 20 % behind are unchanged). The extra room only made the body a smaller part of the clip, so `video-loop --body-height` scaled it up to the delivery height; with 20 % the body is larger in the clip and is scaled down. A 1024 px square still now pads to 2276×1280 instead of 2800×1575. 20 % still holds a weapon raised about a third of the body above the head: the canvas adds at least a quarter of the still's height above it, and the still's own margin over the crown does the rest (8 % of its height is enough). A still cropped at the crown needs `--headroom 0.26` or more for an overhead swing. At this headroom the 16:9 ratio, not `--lead`, sets the width of a square still's canvas. [docs/video-pipeline.md](docs/video-pipeline.md#1-canvas--the-input-frame-decides-the-output-frame).
- **`--shape wide` keeps 35 % above** (`WIDE_OVERRIDE`, no longer the attack row): it is forced on states whose own row is another shape — `video-set --shape wide` across a batch, or a canvas widened after the subject touched a side edge — and a jump is one of them; 35 % on 16:9 keeps about the room a jump's tall row leaves above the still. A forced-wide canvas is the same as before; an attack in a `--shape wide` batch gets that row, as a cheer or a projectile already did.

## v2.21.0 - A clip made by a video MCP on your agent (ZCRE) becomes a loop: the prompt comes out, the mp4 goes in

- **New `sprite-gen video-prompt`** prints the clip prompt `video-set` sends for one (direction, state) — `build_prompt` with `--character`, `--motion` and `--model` — so an agent that makes the clip itself, with a video MCP connected to it, sends the same sentence. `--json` adds the duration (the state's own), whether the clip ends on the canvas (`last_frame`), the loop cut (`cycle`), the canvas, clip, frames and loop steps with placeholders, and for a front or back walk the mid-step redraw (`start_still`). sprite-gen never calls the MCP: the agent uploads the `video-canvas` still, quotes, asks the user, generates and saves the mp4, and `video-frames` and `video-loop` take it from there. [docs/video-pipeline.md](docs/video-pipeline.md#a-clip-from-a-video-mcp-on-your-agent--zcre).
- **ZCRE (지크) is that route's first source**: its remote MCP, signed in with your own ZCRE account and credits, has `grok-imagine-video-1.5` (Grok Imagine Pro) for image-to-video, with no end frame and no Lite model. `--no-last-frame` refuses what `video-set` films pinned — idle, attack, a walk or run in a diagonal — and `--unpinned` films one anyway with a warning (the loop is searched instead of cut whole and may not close). Walk, run and jump from the side, front and back are supported.
- **`video-frames` reads the picture of a clip that carries more**: it takes the first video stream that is not a cover image (`attached_pic`, `stream_index`), and records `codec`, `pix_fmt`, `audio_streams` and `cover_streams`. A ZCRE clip always has an AAC track and an mjpeg cover; the audio is never extracted and the loop outputs carry no sound.
- Measured once end to end: one side walk, 720p 3 s, 52 credits, 33.6 s to generate; the clip was 960×960 H.264 `yuv420p` with an AAC track and a cover, and it cut to a periodic 20-frame loop.
- **The places that moved or scaled a keyed picture with BICUBIC map it with coverage and colour apart** (`transform_cell`, beside `resize_cell` in `sprite_gen/util/resample.py`): the gait fallback's scaled-back frames (`video-loop --anchor motion-auto` on a walk that grows or shrinks through the clip) and a curated move, scale, rotation or shear baked on a smooth row (`curation.apply_transform`, so `compose`, the GIF, the PNG export and the cycle bake). BICUBIC over premultiplied RGBA has the same negative lobe as LANCZOS, smaller, and drew the same lighter rim and key tint at the edge. On the synthetic outlined figure (`tests/keyed_figure.py`) it left 1,088 to 1,864 pixels with a colour none of the four source pixels under them had when a frame was scaled back by 0.84x to 1.19x, and 840 to 2,102 for a half-pixel move, a 10° rotation, a 0.75x shrink, a shear and a 1.3x grow with a 15° rotation; none are left. Coverage keeps BICUBIC's edge, held to the coverage of the 2 x 2 source pixels the colour mixes from; colour is a BILINEAR mix of premultiplied colour, so it is no longer sharpened. A pixel row's curated transform (`fit.pixel_unfake`, NEAREST and re-snapped) and an identity transform are unchanged. The scene GIF's downscale stays LANCZOS: it scales opaque composited frames, where there is no coverage to divide by. ## v2.19.0 - A loop's cells are scaled without a light rim or a green tint at the edge, and front and back stills keep their view

- **`video-loop` scales its cells with coverage and colour taken apart** (`resize_cell`, used by `build_strip`, so the strip, the GIF, the WebP and `video-cycle-align`'s rebuild all get it). ## v2.5.0 - API image generation and smoother loop cuts

- New `openai` image provider: `sprite-gen gen --provider openai` calls the OpenAI Images REST API with nothing but `OPENAI_API_KEY` — the credential a headless container (a Modal worker, CI, a SaaS backend) can have, where the `codex` route's interactive ChatGPT login cannot exist. New images go to `/v1/images/generations`, `--ref` switches to `/v1/images/edits` as multipart with the references as repeated `image[]` parts in order (up to 16), and gpt-image's inline base64 is decoded and published as a verified PNG without resizing. Default model `gpt-image-2.5-flare`. `--transparent` asks for `background: transparent` with `output_format: png` — the same measured `native` strategy as codex, and a live run came back 84 % alpha-0 with the subject at alpha 251–254. `--aspect-ratio` maps to one of the gpt-image `size` values that satisfy the API's constraints (both sides divisible by 16, ratio within 1:3..3:1, 655,360–8,294,400 pixels); a ratio with no exact size is refused rather than rounded to a nearby one you would be billed for. A missing or empty key, a rejected key and a failed request are all terminal: this provider never falls back to codex, to another credential, or to a retry.
- sprite-gen stays subscription-first. `codex` and `grok` run on a subscription you already pay for; `openai` bills per call, so it runs **only** when `--provider openai` names it. `SPRITE_GEN_DEFAULT_PROVIDER=openai` is refused rather than honoured, the guided `workflow` flow neither offers it nor saves it as a preference, no availability fallback targets it (a codex outage still reaches grok, never metered credit), and having `OPENAI_API_KEY` in the environment changes no route by itself. Every call that does spend API credit — image or video — prints one stderr line naming the charge before the request leaves.
- `gen --quality low|medium|high|xhigh|max|auto` and `gen --resolution 1k|1.5k|2k`: the two knobs an image is billed on, as one shared vocabulary with a per-provider subset. grok Imagine carries `auto|low|medium` and all three resolution tiers and prices an image on the pair; openai carries the whole quality range and takes its size from `--aspect-ratio`; codex `image_gen` exposes neither dial. A knob a provider cannot honour fails loudly instead of being dropped from a request body you are about to pay for. Omitting both keeps every existing call byte-identical. The resolution names are tiers, not pixel counts — `1.5k` rendered 1408x1408 at 1:1 (2026-09-20 measurement) — and the grok subsets were read off the server rather than the prose: `quality` deserializes the wider shared enum and then refuses per model (`high` → HTTP 400 "This model only supports the following quality value(s): low, medium, auto."), `resolution: 1.5k` renders although the capability guide lists only 1k and 2k, and an unknown field is not refused at all, which is why these names are checked locally. Grok image reports carry `extra.quality` and `extra.resolution` when the request sent them.
- `video`, `video-extend` and `video-edit` announce the per-call charge on stderr when they run on `XAI_API_KEY` instead of the Grok subscription login — one line per submitted job, naming the duration and resolution it is priced on. The subscription route stays silent and no other video behaviour changed.
- Registering a provider now fails loudly when the workflow catalog has no label for it. The guided flow built its provider choices by zipping labels onto the provider tuple positionally, so a third provider was dropped without a word; labels are a checked mapping (a missing one fails at import) and the flow offers the subscription routes only. `workflow` access probing knows `openai` — ready when the key is set, with `billing: api-credit` and the billing confirmation that goes with it.

- `video-loop` automatic cuts now score the transition actually played from the last displayed frame back to the first. The chooser prefers a step comparable to the cycle's ordinary motion rather than the smallest distance, which could repeat a pose and pause at the wrap. Reports retain `next_frame_distance` as a separate diagnostic.
- `video-frames --spill full` now lowers the tint threshold as well as lifting the cluster-size limit, so faint key-colour reflections are corrected. `--spill auto` examines the reference at the same threshold before selecting full correction, preserving the character's own key-coloured material. The conservative `small` mode and the default 3-second video-set duration are unchanged.

- Gait auto-detection now considers an ambiguous double-period candidate even when the short candidate exceeds the duration floor. It preserves both phase occurrences at source speed when the longer local minimum is similarly credible and passes the periodicity gate; near-exact short repeats, worse doubles, non-gait states and caller window bounds are preserved. Reports identify this conservative choice with `half_period_guard.reason = "ambiguous-harmonic"` and `cycle.review_recommended`, rather than claiming anatomical stride certainty.
- Added a [loop review guide](docs/loop-review.md) defining what numeric gates establish, when sequential visual review is needed, and how to record explicit cut/alignment choices without presenting them as automatic decisions.

## v2.4.1 - A visible hop is no longer refused as "never leaves its rest pose"

- `video-loop` one-shot detection no longer refuses a visible hop because the rest pose is not one pose. The excursion is admitted by either the peak's height in MADs of the rest noise (as before) or the fraction of the subject's pixel mass the peak moves (new, ≥ 0.4); the report records `excursion_moved` and `excursion_rule`. A body that walked a few steps, hopped 42 px and froze scored 1.8 MADs because the walking preamble and the frozen tail inflate the "rest noise"; by moved mass it scores 1.07. A jittering stand still fails both rules, and clips the MAD rule already accepted are cut exactly as before.

## v2.4.0 - Three-second clips and gait windows in seconds

- `video-set --duration` defaults to 3 seconds (was 6). A repeating motion holds two or more cycles at 3 s (walk strides measured at 0.6–1.3 s across seven bodies), a single action is one hop or swing that the one-shot cut already handles, and 6 s bought only a longer wait — and, for jump, more idle standing between hops. `--duration 6` still asks for a longer clip.
- `video-loop` walk and run take their period window in seconds (walk 0.5–1.6 s, run 0.3–1.2 s, capped at half the clip) instead of a fraction of the clip length. At 3 s the old 6–31 % window topped out at 0.96 s and put a 1.0–1.3 s stride out of reach: on four walks generated at 3 s it refused one outright and cut another at a half step; with the bounds in seconds all four pass with equal or better seams, and the 6 s results are unchanged. Idle, jump and attack keep their windows.
- The strip sidecar (`<name>.strip.json`) records `kind` (`periodic` / `one-shot` / `fixed`) and `loop` (`false` for a one-shot), and the spec asset loader reads `loop` from it, so a jump or attack cut as a single action plays once on a trigger instead of repeating. A sidecar without the key is a loop, as before.
- The GIF/WebP never ask for more frames than the strip has cells. A cycle longer than the 64-cell cap is subsampled into the strip; the GIF/WebP are cut from those cells, so requesting the cycle length repeated cells back to back, the writer merged the identical neighbours, and verification refused its own output ("64 frames, expected 68" on a 2.8 s jump cycle).

## v2.3.1 - Key reflections removed from video sprites

## v1.61.0 - Image to Video

- Added `sprite-gen video`: one still + prompt → a verified mp4 through Grok Imagine (`POST /v1/videos/generations`), with duration 1–15 s, 480p/720p/1080p, optional aspect ratio and audio flag, and a `sprite-gen-video-report` JSON.

## v1.60.0 - Native Alpha

- `sprite-gen gen --transparent` now follows a per-provider transparency strategy declared once on each adapter (`Provider.transparency`). `codex` asks `image_gen` for a genuinely transparent background and publishes the measured alpha (`native`, first choice); `grok` keeps deterministic chroma keying because Grok Imagine returns JPEG only.
- Added `--alpha-mode auto|native|chroma`. `auto` reads the provider's strategy, `chroma` forces keying on codex for prompts that already carry a key background, and `native` on a chroma-only provider fails before any model call. With `--ref` attached, `auto` keys instead of asking codex for native alpha (measured 1/6 real alpha with references vs 6/6 chroma); the report records why under `alpha.strategy_source`.
- Native output is verified before publishing: no alpha channel or 0% transparent pixels refuses the run (a drawn checkerboard is never keyed silently), RGB under alpha 0 is scrubbed, and partial alpha is reported untouched.
- Reports carry an `alpha` block (`strategy` plus stats) next to the existing `chroma` stats, and codex's own `transparentBackground` claim under `extra.transparent_background_reported`.
- Sprite-row generation is unchanged: rows still carry the request chroma key and are keyed at extraction.
