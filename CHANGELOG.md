# Changelog

All notable public changes to `sprite-gen` are recorded here. Versions track the `version:` field in `SKILL.md` and `pyproject.toml`.

## Unreleased

- **Output change: `video-follow --on-fold lower` gives each region a gain of its own.** One gain for the strip, set by the region with the smallest radius, let the smallest ellipse decide for every part: a small one (an ear) lowered a large one (a chest) to its own gain, and where that gain went under 1 refused the whole strip, so the chest did not move either. Now a region that folds at `--gain` takes the largest gain that does not fold it (in steps of 0.01), a region that does not fold keeps `--gain`, and one that would have to go under 1, the mass as measured, is **held**: it does not move, and stderr names it by its `--region` (`video-follow: --region <cx,cy,rx,ry> folds at --gain <g>, under 1 (the mass as measured): it is held, and does not move`); a region lowered while another keeps its gain is named the same way (`video-follow: --gain 2.5 folds --region <cx,cy,rx,ry> (a move of <px> px); lowered to --gain <g> for it`). The strip is refused only when every region is held, and that message names the largest region (`… folds a region of radius <r> px over itself (the largest of <n> regions: every one folds), and --on-fold lower would have to go under --gain 1 …`); with one region it reads as before. Where regions of different gains overlap, the larger move wins (`follow.move_regions` takes groups of regions, each with its own offset), and the move there is nowhere steeper than either region's own. In the record each `fold.regions` entry gains `gain` (0 when held) and `held`, the strip's `gain` is the largest any region moved by (`dx_px`, `dy_px` and `reach_px` are that gain's), `fold.ratio` is the nearest any region came to folding and `fold.lowered` says whether any region was lowered or held; the printed summary gains `region_gains`. A strip whose regions all take one gain — one region, regions that fold alike, or none that folds — is written as before, strip, GIF, WebP and record byte for byte. `--on-fold refuse`, the default, is unchanged. [docs/video-pipeline.md](docs/video-pipeline.md#6-follow-through--video-follow).
- **`tests/video/test_follow.py`** moves a chest with a region on the head too small to move at gain 1: before this the strip was refused, now the head region is held and the strip is the chest's alone, byte for byte (and at `--gain 0.9` the chest still moves at 0.9). A head region that folds at 1.75 beside a chest that does not fold takes 1.75 while the chest keeps 2.5, each region's pixels those of the region moved alone at its gain; a small region inside a larger one folds nowhere; a strip whose every region is held is refused untouched; two regions that fold alike are written with the one-gain record and the strip their gain asked for gives; the command names the region it lowered and the one it held.

- **Fix: `video-loop --anchor motion-auto` reads the same motion from the same pixels on every machine.** A patch of its region search that sits on a flat fill matches equally at several offsets; the FFTs that score them round differently between numpy builds (about 1e-14), and that rounding chose the offset. From the same frames a synthetic walk was tracked differently on Linux than on macOS, its distance matrix differed in most entries, and `tests/video/test_loop_prop_seam_held.py` failed on CI only (first choice popping 2.4 there, 11.7 on macOS). Now costs within 1e-9 of the lowest are one match (`auto_motion.MATCH_TIE`) and the offset nearest the centre the search was put on is taken; regions whose qualities tie on that grain are ranked by their box; and the drift lines that move the analysis frames are least-squares lines with exactly rounded sums (`auto_motion._line`), not `np.polyfit`, whose LAPACK solve rounded differently and tipped bilinear samples of those frames. The tracked offsets, the analysis frames and the distance matrix are now bit-identical on macOS arm64 and Linux x86-64; only the last digits of reported costs and scores differ. Footage whose patches never tie is cut as before: the same regions, offsets and cut, its analysis translations moved only in their last bits (about 1e-14), which can tip a bilinear sample and move a score in its sixth decimal. Drawn, flat-filled frames, where ties are the rule, are tracked — and may be cut — otherwise than v2.36.0 tracked them on any one machine.
- **`tests/video/test_auto_motion.py`** perturbs every FFT correlation by up to 1e-12 of its largest value, seed by seed: the tied offset taken on the flat torso, the regions found and every tracked offset are the same under all of them (under v2.36.0 eight seeds took six offsets, 14 px apart, and four seeds found four different region pairs), and the drift line is `np.polyfit`'s to 1e-12 and bit-identical in any order of its points. `test_a_held_part_that_swings_with_the_step_is_cut_one_step_long` draws its pennant on a 53-frame beat (phase 0.75): its first choice pops 12.6, no other candidate's top over 8.5, the cut taken closes below the hand at 0.0; on v2.35.0 with this fix it fails where v2.35.0 reads nothing below the top.

## v2.36.0 - A slow walk cut one step long is named, and cut again on a count; a staff that closes only on two steps is cut as one cycle

- **New record: `cycle.step_screen` on every walk and run `video-loop --anchor motion-auto` cuts.** A walk slower than its window holds (0.5 to 1.6 s, at most half the clip) is cut one step long where its legs are drawn alike: the step repeats in pixels, the loop walks on one leg, its arms jump at the wrap, and a set aligned with it plays one step where its other directions play two. Nothing said so — the two-cycle screen looks only below the cut. Now the cut is screened the other way, over the whole clip (`period.step_screen`): the lag within 3 frames of twice the cut that repeats best (`lag`, `searched`), its distance over the cut's own over the same frames (`ratio`, `pairs`), its dip under the clip's profile (`depth`), how much of a second cycle the clip holds past it (`coverage`), and the step signal's peaks over the cut (`leg_peaks`, a record only). A repeat there (`depth` at least 0.15) better than at the cut (`ratio` at most **0.85**, `period.STEP_RATIO_MAX`) is a `suspect`. **Nothing is cut on it**: a cycle on a frame count that is not whole repeats better at its double too, so only a count of the steps settles it. Images, `strip.json`, warnings and stderr are as before, byte for byte; the report's `cycle` and the printed summary gain `step_screen`. [docs/loop-repair.md](docs/loop-repair.md#the-step-screen--a-slow-walk-cut-one-step-long).
- **New option: `video-loop --steps 1|2`** (with `--anchor motion-auto`), the count of steps in the cut it takes without it, from whoever looked at the loop. `1` cuts the walk again in the window [2L − 1, 2L + 1] — not bounded by half the clip, but held inside an explicit `--min-len`/`--max-len` (below) — with the same search, the equivalent cut whose wrap the clip itself plays taken (`choice: "closure"`) and no window twice that; a clip too short to see that window repeat is cut all the same, the count standing for the repeat (`method: "counted-window-v1"`, `unseen`). `2` keeps the cut. Either way `cycle.steps` records the count (`by: "given"`, `given`, and for `1` `first_cut` and `window`), `strip.json` says `steps: 2`, and `cycle.coverage` is (n − L) / L. **New warning**: a cut two steps long whose clip holds under 0.25 of a cycle past it (`loop.COVERAGE_MIN`) — `the cut is two steps long (… frames, one cycle) and the clip holds … of a cycle past it (under 0.25): its repeat is barely seen — the seam says whether it closes; a longer take shows it repeat`. It is cut, not refused; the seam gate judges it.
- **Output change: an explicit `--min-len`/`--max-len` window is the window, on every path that sets a cut's length.** `--anchor motion-auto` cut the caller's ceiling back to half the clip, so asking for a slow walk's cycle (`--min-len 41 --max-len 43` on 73 frames) searched [41, 36] and failed. Only the state's default window is bounded by half the clip now. The window is worked out once (`loop.CutWindow`) and every search reads its own off it — the first search, a window two steps long, the count (`--steps 1`), the gait fallback, the one-shot failover — so none sets a bound of its own: the `--cycle auto` one-shot failover of an action state widened an explicit `--max-len` to nine tenths of the clip (a 16-frame hop asked under `--max-len 14` was cut 36 long) and the one-shot search took no `--min-len` at all; both are held to the caller's bounds now (`detect_one_shot` honours `min_len`; `run_loop` passes `ONE_SHOT_MIN_LEN` unless `--min-len` is given, so a run without one cuts as before), and a one-shot the bounds leave no room for says so (`no complete one-shot return … — in 8-14 frames, inside --min-len 8 --max-len 14`). Whatever chose the cut, `--cycle fixed` and `pinned` included, a cut outside an explicit bound fails: `the cut is … frames long (…), outside --max-len N`. **A count and a window with no length in common fail**: `--steps 1` on a 21-frame step under `--max-len 25` cut 42 frames; now `--steps 1: the cut of 21 frames from 18 is one step, so the cycle is 41-43 frames long, and --max-len 25 leaves none of that: the count and the window disagree — drop one, or give a window that holds the cycle` (exit 1, the failed report's `cycle.steps` with `counted_window`, `window: null` and `refused`). Where they share part of the window, only that part is read (`cycle.steps.window`, `counted_window` beside it). A run with no explicit bound cuts byte for byte as before.
- **Output change: once the top of the silhouette jumps at its first choice, `video-loop --anchor motion-auto` reads the held part below its top too, and may cut two steps long — one cycle.** A staff turns about the hand as the arm swings, once a cycle; one step on it is back in place at the top band moving the other way. Where the legs are drawn alike the search takes that step as the cycle: v2.35.0 chose again on the top band alone, took such a cut — the top closed — and the shaft under the hand swung back at the wrap, with no warning. Now, behind the seam pop only, the side of the body the top band sits on is read row by row on the silhouette's outer edge (`repair.held_side`, `repair.side_edges`, `repair.held_edge` on the frames the search chooses from, `loop.HeldEdge`): per row, the cut's first and last frames against the clip's frames after its last and before its first, in place and in speed, over the row's median step in the loop; the 80th percentile over the rows is the held seam, closing under **1.2** (`repair.HELD_EDGE_REFERENCE`). Among the candidates whose top does not jump, one whose held side closes is taken (the usual 15 % of the best score, then the smallest held seam). Where none closes, windows twice a candidate's length are read — two steps of a confirmed repeat, one cycle, not themselves seen repeating — and one whose held side closes and whose top does not jump is cut: `cycle.steps` is `{"count": 2, "by": "held-edge", "step_lengths": [...]}` (the candidates it is two of, each one step), `cycle.period_local` its own length, `cycle.coverage` recorded, and `strip.json` says `steps: 2`. A cut with fewer than two clip frames either side of it cannot be read: `skipped`, counted under `unread`, never chosen. An explicit `--max-len` stays the cut's ceiling (`two_step_capped`: `max_len`, `lengths_over`), and counted steps read nothing twice as long. `cycle.seam_pop.held_edge` records `side`, `reference`, `measured`, `unread`, `doubled`, `chosen` and `two_step` (`read`, lengths tried, `measured`, `unread`, `top_pops`, `closing`); a candidate row carries `held_edge` (`null` with `held_edge_skipped`). A first choice whose top does not jump reads nothing new. [docs/loop-repair.md](docs/loop-repair.md#the-held-side--a-staff-that-closes-at-the-top-and-swings-below-the-hand).
- **New warning: a held part that no window one or two lengths long closes.** The top band's cut stands and `jolt.warnings` and stderr gain `the held part does not close at the wrap on the <side> side of the silhouette, below its top: …x its rows' median step (over 1.2); no window one or two lengths long closes it — it swings on a beat of its own` (or, where `--max-len` kept the longer windows from being read, `no window one length long closes it, and windows two lengths long (…-… frames) are over --max-len N, so they were not read`; where the steps were counted, `no window of the counted length closes it`); a held side that could not be read is said as well. Nothing fails on it.
- **Output change: `video-cycle-align` reads a loop's steps, and stops on a loop that may be one step.** A loop whose `strip.json` says `steps: 2` returns half way at its second step: that suspect is recorded `status: "steps"` (with `steps`) and does not stop the set, and the loop is resampled to the set's length like the rest; `--cycles <loop>=2` on it takes one half out and drops `steps`. A loop whose cut's report (beside the loop, or one directory up for a `video-set` item: `align.cut_report`) has `cycle.step_screen.suspect`, and whose steps nobody counted, is a suspect too (`one_step`: `length`, `lag`, `seconds`, `ratio`, `depth`, `pairs`, `coverage`, `leg_peaks`, `why`, `report`): stopped under `--multi-cycle fail` with its name and `cut it again with sprite-gen video-loop … --steps 1`, aligned and warned about under `warn`; `--cycles <loop>=1` says it holds a whole cycle. Every loop with neither is aligned as before, its row, `strip.json` and cells byte for byte.
- **`video-set`**: an item whose cut's steps are known carries `loop.steps`, one the step screen suspects `loop.step_screen` (`lag`, `ratio`, `depth`, `coverage`), and `table.md` says so in the cycle column (`46 (2 steps)`, `21 (one step?)`); a state stopped on a loop that may be one step says `<item> may be one step (cut it again with video-loop --steps 1 if it is)`. A cut two steps long now carries `ratio` like any candidate, which the item reads.
- **`tests/video/test_explicit_window.py`** holds one table of every path that sets a length — the first search, a window two steps long, the count, the gait fallback, the one-shot failover, a fixed cut — under an explicit `--max-len` or `--min-len`, partial windows included (a held staff read only at 47 of 47-49 frames, a count read at 41-42 of 41-43): each run cuts inside the bounds or fails, and the report shows the path ran. Before this, the count (25 → 42), the one-shot failover (14 and 30 → 36) and the fixed cut (30 → 40) were cut outside them.
- **`tests/video/test_one_step_cut.py`** draws a side walker with a 42-frame cycle, legs alike and arms shaded apart, over 73 frames: v2.35.0 cuts it at 21 and says nothing; now the cut is the same and the step screen names it (`ratio` 0.48), the same drawing on 25 and 23.5 frames is written byte for byte as with no screen (23.5 a suspect that is a cycle), `--steps 1` cuts 15/42 where the arm's wrap is 0 against 7.6 for the step, `--steps 2` keeps the cut, a 50-frame clip is cut 8/42 and warned about its coverage, `--min-len 41 --max-len 43` is the window, and the set alignment stops on the step and aligns its re-cut. **`tests/video/test_loop_prop_seam_held.py`** draws a walker whose legs come back every 24 frames with a staff turning about the hand every 48: every cut one step long whose top closes reads a held seam of 2.0 to 5.2, and the window two steps long taken reads 0 and is aligned at the set's length as one cycle. A staff swinging with the step below a pennant that sways on its own beat stays one step long; a staff whose foot swings every 37 frames is kept on the top band's cut and warned about; a walk without a staff reads nothing below its top; two loops without a staff are aligned with exactly the `strip.json` and `cycle_align` keys v2.35.0 wrote.

## v2.35.0 - A staff that swings on its own beat is not cut where it jumps

- **Output change: `video-loop --anchor motion-auto` chooses its cut again when the top of the silhouette jumps into the first frame.** A staff, a flag or a raised spear held above the head can sway on a beat slower than the steps; a cut one step long then ends with it elsewhere, the body closes and the held part jumps at the wrap. The seam ratio is an area measure and a thin rod hardly moves it, so such a cut passed. The search now weighs, for its first choice, the top band's step into the first frame over the loop's median step (`repair.seam_pop`, on the frames it chooses from, moved by the analysis translation; the median a pixel at least), counted only when the wrap is that band's largest step. Over the bound (**10**, `repair.SEAM_POP_REFERENCE`) every candidate is measured, and among those under it the cut is taken from the scores within the usual 15 % of the best, smallest jump first. The report's `cycle.seam_pop` records `first_choice`, `applied`, `measured`, `unread` and `chosen` (or `why` when every candidate read jumps and the first choice is kept). A cut whose top band cannot be read — a frame with nothing in it — has no jump to rank: it is recorded `skipped` (why) in place of a `pop` (`first_choice.skipped`; a candidate row's `seam_pop` is `null` with `seam_pop_skipped`), counted under `unread`, and never chosen as one that closes; a first choice that cannot be read is kept. A first choice under the bound is kept and nothing past it is measured: a loop without such a part is cut, written and warned about byte for byte as before. [docs/loop-repair.md](docs/loop-repair.md#the-seam-pop--a-part-held-above-the-head-swinging-on-its-own-beat).
- **New: every walk and run loop reports the seam pop and warns on it** (`jolt.seam_pop`: per axis `wrap_pct`, `step_median_pct`, `wrap_over_median`, `wrap_is_largest`; `pop`, `reference`, `pops`; `seam_pop` in the printed summary's `jolt`). Over the bound, on the loop as it plays, `jolt.warnings` and stderr gain `the top of the silhouette jumps into the loop's first frame up or down … (over 10x): a part held above the head swings on its own beat and does not close at this cut`. The top line (up or down) had no bound at all; a sideways jump into frame 0 the head's 0.75 % bound already names is not said twice. Nothing fails on it, and a fixed or `--cycle periodic` cut is not chosen again.
- **New: a motion-auto search that finds no cycle reports the windows it measured and refused.** The failed report's `cycle` was `null`, so a caller that delivers anyway (a forced `--cycle fixed`) had no measured window and took the whole clip — which, for a walk, cannot loop. It now carries `status: "refused"`, `window`, `refused_count` and up to 12 `candidates` (`start`, `length`, `score`, `ratio`, `context_repeat_over_step`, `drift_line_residual_analysis_px`, `seam_pop`, and `refused`: why), best score first, those whose top could not be read (`seam_pop: null`, `seam_pop_skipped`) after those whose top closes, and those whose top jumps at the wrap last (`local_cycle.REFUSED_SHOWN`). The error, the exit code and every other gate are unchanged. [docs/loop-review.md](docs/loop-review.md#the-gait-fallback-a-slow-walk-or-a-walk-toward-the-camera).
- **`tests/video/test_loop_prop_seam.py`** draws a walker stepping every 24 frames with a staff above its head swaying every 61: v2.34.0 cuts it where the staff's tip jumps 11 times its largest step inside the loop, and now it is cut where the tip closes; a fixed cut whose staff tip rises and drops back at the wrap warns; a walker whose staff tip is flung out of the top band in one frame is not cut on a window holding that frame, which cannot be read (the branch's first take recorded those as jumping 0 and chose one whose tip jumps 8 times its largest step); a walk whose legs never come back fails with measured windows in its report.

## v2.34.0 - A foot the set alignment names by too small a margin is left for whoever looks

- **Output change: a side or diagonal loop whose shade parts its two strikes by less than 0.025 in luma is no longer named** (`video-cycle-align`, `video-set --align-cycles`; `align.SHADE_MARGIN`, was 0.015). The shade cue (the far leg drawn darker) named a foot whenever the strikes differed by 0.015; on drawn walks checked by eye it named feet wrong at margins under the new bar and right above it. Its frames, strip, GIF and WebP are those of its larger strike as before; only who named the foot changes. Front and back loops (the depth cue, 1 % of the body's height) and every loop whose margin clears the bar come out byte for byte as they were. [docs/loop-repair.md](docs/loop-repair.md#4-one-cycle-for-a-direction-set--video-cycle-align-video-set---align-cycles-auto).
- **`foot_why` of a margin too small starts `low-margin`**, with the margin, the cue and the bar (`low-margin: the two strikes differ by 0.0235 in shade, under the 0.025 it takes to name a foot`; `align.LOW_MARGIN`), in place of `the two strikes look alike in shade (…)`, for both cues — in the loop row, `unnamed_feet`, the warning, and `foot_unnamed_why` once told.

- New `openai` image provider: `sprite-gen gen --provider openai` calls the OpenAI Images REST API with nothing but `OPENAI_API_KEY` — the credential a headless container (a Modal worker, CI, a SaaS backend) can have, where the `codex` route's interactive ChatGPT login cannot exist. New images go to `/v1/images/generations`, `--ref` switches to `/v1/images/edits` as multipart with the references as repeated `image[]` parts in order (up to 16), and gpt-image's inline base64 is decoded and published as a verified PNG without resizing. Default model `gpt-image-2.5-flare`. `--transparent` asks for `background: transparent` with `output_format: png` — the same measured `native` strategy as codex, and a live run came back 84 % alpha-0 with the subject at alpha 251–254. `--aspect-ratio` maps to one of the gpt-image `size` values that satisfy the API's constraints (both sides divisible by 16, ratio within 1:3..3:1, 655,360–8,294,400 pixels); a ratio with no exact size is refused rather than rounded to a nearby one you would be billed for. A missing or empty key, a rejected key and a failed request are all terminal: this provider never falls back to codex, to another credential, or to a retry.
- sprite-gen stays subscription-first. `codex` and `grok` run on a subscription you already pay for; `openai` bills per call, so it runs **only** when `--provider openai` names it. `SPRITE_GEN_DEFAULT_PROVIDER=openai` is refused rather than honoured, the guided `workflow` flow neither offers it nor saves it as a preference, no availability fallback targets it (a codex outage still reaches grok, never metered credit), and having `OPENAI_API_KEY` in the environment changes no route by itself. Every call that does spend API credit — image or video — prints one stderr line naming the charge before the request leaves.
- `gen --quality low|medium|high|xhigh|max|auto` and `gen --resolution 1k|1.5k|2k`: the two knobs an image is billed on, as one shared vocabulary with a per-provider subset. grok Imagine carries `auto|low|medium` and all three resolution tiers and prices an image on the pair; openai carries the whole quality range and takes its size from `--aspect-ratio`; codex `image_gen` exposes neither dial. A knob a provider cannot honour fails loudly instead of being dropped from a request body you are about to pay for. Omitting both keeps every existing call byte-identical. The resolution names are tiers, not pixel counts — `1.5k` rendered 1408x1408 at 1:1 (2026-09-20 measurement) — and the grok subsets were read off the server rather than the prose: `quality` deserializes the wider shared enum and then refuses per model (`high` → HTTP 400 "This model only supports the following quality value(s): low, medium, auto."), `resolution: 1.5k` renders although the capability guide lists only 1k and 2k, and an unknown field is not refused at all, which is why these names are checked locally. Grok image reports carry `extra.quality` and `extra.resolution` when the request sent them.
- `video`, `video-extend` and `video-edit` announce the per-call charge on stderr when they run on `XAI_API_KEY` instead of the Grok subscription login — one line per submitted job, naming the duration and resolution it is priced on. The subscription route stays silent and no other video behaviour changed.
- Registering a provider now fails loudly when the workflow catalog has no label for it. The guided flow built its provider choices by zipping labels onto the provider tuple positionally, so a third provider was dropped without a word; labels are a checked mapping (a missing one fails at import) and the flow offers the subscription routes only. `workflow` access probing knows `openai` — ready when the key is set, with `billing: api-credit` and the billing confirmation that goes with it.

- `video-loop` automatic cuts now score the transition actually played from the last displayed frame back to the first. The chooser prefers a step comparable to the cycle's ordinary motion rather than the smallest distance, which could repeat a pose and pause at the wrap. Reports retain `next_frame_distance` as a separate diagnostic.
- `video-frames --spill full` now lowers the tint threshold as well as lifting the cluster-size limit, so faint key-colour reflections are corrected. `--spill auto` examines the reference at the same threshold before selecting full correction, preserving the character's own key-coloured material. The conservative `small` mode and the default 3-second video-set duration are unchanged.

- Gait auto-detection now considers an ambiguous double-period candidate even when the short candidate exceeds the duration floor. It preserves both phase occurrences at source speed when the longer local minimum is similarly credible and passes the periodicity gate; near-exact short repeats, worse doubles, non-gait states and caller window bounds are preserved. Reports identify this conservative choice with `half_period_guard.reason = "ambiguous-harmonic"` and `cycle.review_recommended`, rather than claiming anatomical stride certainty.
- Added a [loop review guide](docs/loop-review.md) defining what numeric gates establish, when sequential visual review is needed, and how to record explicit cut/alignment choices without presenting them as automatic decisions.

## v2.4.1 - A visible hop is no longer refused as "never leaves its rest pose"

- `video-loop` one-shot detection no longer refuses a visible hop because the rest pose is not one pose. The excursion is admitted by either the peak's height in MADs of the rest noise (as before) or the fraction of the subject's pixel mass the peak moves (new, ≥ 0.4); the report records `excursion_moved` and `excursion_rule`. A body that walked a few steps, hopped 42 px and froze scored 1.8 MADs because the walking preamble and the frozen tail inflate the "rest noise"; by moved mass it scores 1.07. A jittering stand still fails both rules, and clips the MAD rule already accepted are cut exactly as before.

## v2.4.0 - Three-second clips and gait windows in seconds

- `video-set --duration` defaults to 3 seconds (was 6). A repeating motion holds two or more cycles at 3 s (walk strides measured at 0.6–1.3 s across seven bodies), a single action is one hop or swing that the one-shot cut already handles, and 6 s bought only a longer wait — and, for jump, more idle standing between hops. `--duration 6` still asks for a longer clip.
- `video-loop` walk and run take their period window in seconds (walk 0.5–1.6 s, run 0.3–1.2 s, capped at half the clip) instead of a fraction of the clip length. At 3 s the old 6–31 % window topped out at 0.96 s and put a 1.0–1.3 s stride out of reach: on four walks generated at 3 s it refused one outright and cut another at a half step; with the bounds in seconds all four pass with equal or better seams, and the 6 s results are unchanged. Idle, jump and attack keep their windows.
- The strip sidecar (`<name>.strip.json`) records `kind` (`periodic` / `one-shot` / `fixed`) and `loop` (`false` for a one-shot), and the spec asset loader reads `loop` from it, so a jump or attack cut as a single action plays once on a trigger instead of repeating. A sidecar without the key is a loop, as before.
- The GIF/WebP never ask for more frames than the strip has cells. A cycle longer than the 64-cell cap is subsampled into the strip; the GIF/WebP are cut from those cells, so requesting the cycle length repeated cells back to back, the writer merged the identical neighbours, and verification refused its own output ("64 frames, expected 68" on a 2.8 s jump cycle).

## v2.3.1 - Key reflections removed from video sprites

- `video-frames --spill small|full|auto` and `video-set --spill` (default `auto`): remove the key colour a video model paints into the subject — a reflection across metal, a tint on a pale surface — which the chroma engine's trapped-spill pass leaves alone once the patch is larger than its small-cluster cap. `auto` keys the still the clip was made from and measures its own key-coloured material; none worth the name means every key tint in the clip is spill (`full`), otherwise the still-pipeline rule stays (`small`). Colour only — alpha is unchanged — and colours without key tint are untouched. `video-frames` defaults to `small`, byte-identical to before; the frames report records the decision under `spill`.
- Known limitation: on pixels where the reflection is removed, colour can lean warm. The correction solves observed = (1-k)·subject + k·key for the subject and so scales the unkeyed channels by 1/(1-k), which amplifies any warm light already mixed in (a pale armour piece can come out slightly reddish instead of neutral silver). A correction that keeps the source still's palette is open for contributions.
